# Allocating the two signs across cumulative height bounds

Let \(t_i\) be the complete height grid from the [Green proof](../../round8/zero_strip/COMPLETE_GREEN.md). Denote by \(C_i^+\) an upper bound for the cumulative weighted sum with factor \((\cos(\omega\gamma))_+\), and by \(C_i^a\) the corresponding absolute-cosine bound. Each includes all real-part nodes through \(7/8\). The same \(C_i^+\) bounds \((-\cos(\omega\gamma))_+\). Indeed replace the angle by its translate by \(\pi\) in every Green comparison. Source positivity and angular domination are invariant, the odd Fourier coefficients change sign, the Poisson costs use their absolute values, and the horizontal sine factors still vanish on the same physical edges. This symmetry also holds for the raw jointly priced cones. The cumulative prices use the same assumptions as the [raw comparison](../../round8/zero_strip/RAW_GREEN.md).

Let \(P_i,N_i\) bound the positive and negative parts of the actual undamped centered spatial transform on the height band ending at \(t_i\). The argument of the transform ranges over the whole closed physical slab. For the first band take its lower endpoint to be zero. The infinite tail separately pays a common factor \(e\) for every lower root, and pays \(e(t_{\rm end}/\gamma)^2\) above the upper height. In the original polygon take \(e\) from its inverse-square bound. It suffices for nonnegative numbers \(u_i,v_i,w_i\) to satisfy
\[
 \sum_{j\ge i}(u_j+w_j)\ge(P_i-e)_+,\qquad
 \sum_{j\ge i}(v_j+w_j)\ge(N_i-e)_+.
\]
For each zero multiply the first inequality by its positive cosine and the second by its negative cosine. Adding gives the pointwise spectral majorization since their sum is the absolute cosine. Sum over the true zeros. The additional cost on the positive ordinates is at most \(\sum_i[(u_i+v_i)C_i^++w_iC_i^a]\). The damped transform error, verified zeros, prime powers, and integer endpoint correction are paid in full. There is no independence assumption on different heights or on either sign.

[The height solver](height_allocation.py) uses a sparse linear program to propose coefficients. Round them to nonnegative dyadic numbers and process the windows downwards, adding any excess right-hand side to the matching one-sign cost. This gives an explicit outward-checked feasible majorant even if the proposal is poor. [Transform bounds](signed_profile.py) use separate signed endpoint extrema with interpolation curvature on every frequency cell, without replacing the entire positive envelope by its absolute value. At \([139.70,139.71]\), a trial with 384 height bands and the multi-source comparisons gave total cost below .9861.

For a separately proposed symmetric piecewise linear density with half-grid values \(h_0,\ldots,h_n\), \(h_n=0\), \(h_j\ge0\), set \(B=2\sum h_j-h_0>0\). On the unit spatial interval its mass-normalized maximum is \(2n\max h_j/B\). Its centered transform is
\[
 \operatorname{sinc}^2(y/(4n))\,[h_0+2\sum_{j\ge1}h_j\cos(jy/(2n))]/B.
\]
The summed absolute coefficients of its second derivative are \(V=(2n)^2\sum |d_j|/B\), with \(d_0=2(h_1-h_0)\) and \(d_j=2(h_{j-1}-2h_j+h_{j+1})\) for \(j\ge1\), taking \(h_{n+1}=0\). Two integrations by parts give an upper bound \(V/y^2\) for the damped transform as well: on the uncentered unit interval the damping has modulus at most one. Use \(e=V/(d_{\min}t_{\rm end})^2\). The cumulative prices depend only on the real-part weights and the physical cosine and can therefore be reused. [The joint polygon search](joint_polygon.py) proposes heights simultaneously with the cumulative allocations; all heights are rounded to nonnegative integers, the total mass is recomputed exactly, and both spectral signs, the prime-power coefficient and the inverse-square tail are re-evaluated for those integers.

Thirteen consecutive closed cells from \(139.29\) to \(139.55\) have maximum complete upper endpoint below **.999098590209973**, using the two sign allocations and the raw many-source comparison. This supplies another connection into the published-in-worktree chain at \(139.55\).

## Signed interior densities and translated heights

Suppose the target contains no prime. Every prime in the open support of an interior polygon is absent, independent of the signs of its heights. Its endpoint values vanish. In the compact explicit formula the pole has exactly the polygon integral. Bound the finite damping and trivial-zero terms using its absolute integral, at most \( (2\sum |h_j|-|h_0|)/B \), and the prime-power sum using its absolute peak. The piecewise linear formula for the transform and its inverse-square tail still hold. At very small frequencies use \(|\hat\phi(y)-\hat\phi(0)|\le y^2\int v^2|\phi(v)|,dv/2\). The verified low zeros have their own separate cost, so the first unverified band may start at the actual height \(H\).

The translated tables from the near-height proof can be inserted between any two cumulative heights. Transport a nonnegative real-part reward from \(l_0\) to \(l\) by multiplying by \(\max(e^{-(l-l_0)},e^{-(l-l_0)/8})\). A signed-cosine row additionally needs its physical frequency window to contain the entire physical slab. Merge equal heights termwise by minimum, then propagate all cumulative upper bounds backwards. The [early source table](../../round8/zero_strip/early_green_rows.py) computes seven full moment pairs and their horizontal errors on eight half-unit slabs between log heights 29 and 33. With the original Green estimates and the early and translated tables, exploratory full costs at the left endpoints 115.42, 116, 120 and 130 were respectively below 1.862, 1.977, 1.963 and 1.339 for widths .01. All four exceeded one.

The [mixed test](mixed_physical.py) adds a signed interior polygon to a positive multiple of the [Logan test](../physical/COMPANION_KERNEL.md). Its common pole normalization is the sum of their nominal masses. Under the empty-interval hypothesis only the Logan part pays the exterior primes. Both normalized zero sums have phase \(\cos((L+d/2)\gamma)\), where \(d=\log(1+4e^{-L/4})\). For the symmetric Logan formula, pair \(\beta\) with \(1-\beta\) at the same ordinate to get the same factor \(e^{(\beta-1)(L+d/2)}\). Its complex Gaussian remainder is charged separately by its Lipschitz constant summed over all physical zeros, with the old Gaussian tail after the last tabulated height. The compact damping error uses the signed density's absolute integral.

Inside the overlap, sum the two real spectra *before* taking either sign. Split at the Logan band edge. On each smooth interval interpolate the combined spectrum using the sum of both second-derivative bounds. Moving the polygon's frequency from the mid-width to the actual width costs at most its absolute integral times the upper ordinate times one quarter of the width range, since its centered support is contained in \([-1/2,1/2]\). Outside the band use the polynomial transform and its own inverse-square tail. The positive and negative parts of the resulting combined upper envelopes then use the same cumulative-height allocation and arithmetic checks as above.
