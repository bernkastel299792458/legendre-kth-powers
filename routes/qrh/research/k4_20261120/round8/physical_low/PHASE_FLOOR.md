# A physical test of the middle Mellin frequency, and an exact resummation

Use the constants and physical spline \(F\) of [the remaining signed band](REMAINING_PHASE.md), and set \(D=\lfloor z/11\rfloor\). Select \(q=pr\) with distinct primes \(D<p,r\le2D\). These moduli lie strictly between \(e^{70.7}\) and \(X/23\). All their admissible pairs have true gcd 1 and \(\lambda_{pr}=0\) since \(pr>z\). Hence \(B(q)=2|\lambda_p\lambda_r|\), with \(G_{210}(z)|\lambda_p|=\rho(p)\) because \(z/p<11\).

On the specific subband \(H/(16\pi)\le\kappa=nX/(210q)\le H/(8\pi)\), the actual sum of complex absolutized terms in the normalization of (1) exceeds **66.54**. Taking the real part of each Fourier term before the absolute value still gives more than **2.94**. In contrast, the total contribution of these same moduli to the *entire signed middle band* is bounded above by **.000280677** by recombining their frequencies. The two estimates concern the same weight and all possible prime choices in that square. The latter is a one-sided estimate.

**A true lower bound.** Put \(b=2\pi-a\) and \(t_c=e^c\). The two original first lobes, restricted to \(H(c-116)\le |u|\le b\), have total physical area exceeding \(.9527363x/H\); the lower right-endpoint quadrature is justified by the two decreasing sinc factors in [the first-lobe proof](SPIKE_FLOOR.md). Recenter \(\widehat F(n/(210q))\) by multiplying by \(e^{-2\pi i nt_c/(210q)}\). On those lobes the phase deviation is at most \(\theta=(H/4)e^{-2r}(e^{b/H}-1)<\pi/2\). Since \(F\ge W\) and \(\int F\le XA/H+2\delta S\), the real part after recentering exceeds .2499462 in units \(x/H\). To bound its imaginary part, pair the two original lobes at \(\pm u\). Their physical densities differ by at most \((e^c/H)(2b/H)e^{b/H}\). The two sine arguments add to at most \((H/4)(b/H)^2e^{b/H}\), by the Taylor remainder for \(\cosh\). The remaining nonnegative mass is at most \(\int F\) minus the mass of the original lobes. Altogether the centered imaginary part has magnitude less than \(.104836x/H\).

To count the selected primes, use \(\binom{2D}{D}\ge4^D/(2D+1)\). The primes \(\le\sqrt{2D}\) contribute at most \((2D)^{\sqrt{2D}}\) by the factorial valuation formula; the primes between \(\sqrt{2D}\) and \(2D/3\) contribute at most \(4^{2D/3}\). Indeed \(\prod_{p\le m}p\le4^m\) by induction using the central binomial coefficient for \(m=2j\), and \(\binom{2j+1}j\le4^j\) for the primes \(j+1<p\le2j+1\). The primes \(2D/3<p\le D\) have valuation zero in \(\binom{2D}{D}\). It follows that the prime count in the selected interval exceeds \(.00820847D\). Each selected modulus has at least \((H/(16\pi))210D^2/X-1>10^{11}\) integers \(n\) in the indicated band. Use \(\sum B(q)/q\ge M(M-1)/(4D^2G_+^2)\), where \(M\) is the lower prime count just proved and \(G_+=\log z+1.34+2.44/\sqrt z\). As \(Q\) is squarefree, all \(|c_Q(n)|\ge1\).

To retain real terms, omit pairs with \(|t_c/(210pr)-1/2|\le10^{-5}\). For any fixed outer prime, their inner primes belong to an interval of length at most
\[
 \frac{t_c}{210D}\frac{2\cdot10^{-5}}{1/4-10^{-10}} .
\]
Charge all integers there. Elsewhere \(.14<\alpha=t_c/(210pr)<.58\) and \(|\sin(2\pi\alpha)|\ge4\cdot10^{-5}\). For consecutive \(n\), the geometric sum bounds the discrepancy of \(\sum\cos^2(2\pi n\alpha)\) from \(N/2\) by \(1/(8\cdot10^{-5})\). Thus more than \((N(1/2-.65^2)-1/(8\cdot10^{-5}))/(1-.65^2)\) terms, with a weak inequality sufficient, have \(|\cos(2\pi n\alpha)|\ge.65\). Each uncentered real Fourier coefficient there exceeds \(.0827966x/H\) in absolute value by the two centered estimates above. This proves the claimed normalized lower bounds, showing that the Mellin-scale range \(2\pi\kappa\in[H/8,H/4]\) has physically significant amplitudes, even beyond the earlier \(e^{26}\) limit.

**Recombination.** Recombine all positive and negative Fourier modes for these same \(q\), and drop their nonnegative zero-mode subtraction for an upper bound. Only the integers \(k\) with \((k,210)=1,\ e^{c-2r}/(4D^2)<k\le X/D^2\) can contribute; there are 21, with \(k\le121\). At a fixed \(k\) the outer prime belongs to
\[
 p_0=\max(D,e^{c-2r}/(2kD))<p\le p_1=\min(2D,X/(kD)).
\]
The inner prime in each physical interval of length \(y\) lies in a span of length \(y/(kp)\). For all \(V\ge0\) its count is bounded by \(2V/\log T+T+1\), using the interval Brun–Titchmarsh theorem for \(V\ge T\) and the trivial bound for smaller \(V\). Take \(T=e^{22}\) inside and \(T=D^{.92}\) outside. Partial summation of the affine outer bound gives \(\sum_{p_0<p\le p_1}1/p\le 2\log(p_1/p_0)/\log T+(T+1)/p_0\). Integrate these slopes and intercepts against the same spline majorization. The actual-multiple contribution is below .000229514. The modes with \(\kappa\le1\) for this family cost at most .000051163 in absolute value, by its squared harmonic upper bound and \(\sum_{n\le 4\cdot210D^2/X}|c_Q(n)|\). The upper diagonal tail has its earlier absolute bound. This proves the signed middle-band upper bound, without estimating individual middle Fourier modes. The interval sieve inequality is [Montgomery and Vaughan, *The large sieve*, Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300004708).
